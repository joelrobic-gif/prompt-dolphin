# V1 Archetype List

> **Ten archetypes for the MVP launch.** Five generic — table stakes, every knowledge worker needs them. Five domain-specific to regulated industries — the GTM wedge that no marketplace competitor has.

---

## Generic archetypes (table stakes)

### 1. Executive email / communication

**Use case:** Draft an email to a senior leader, board member, or external counterparty where tone and precision matter.

**Spine emphasis:** role (executive communications director), format (subject line + 3-paragraph body), exclusions (no hedging, no consultancy-speak).

**Example input:** "Email to my CEO explaining why we should pause the Q4 product launch."

---

### 2. Meeting preparation brief

**Use case:** Prep notes for a one-hour meeting — background, objectives, key questions, decision framework.

**Spine emphasis:** context (attendees + history), format (one-page brief), critique ("would I walk in feeling prepared?").

**Example input:** "Prep me for tomorrow's QBR with our biggest customer."

---

### 3. Research synthesis

**Use case:** Synthesize 3–10 sources into a coherent argument or summary. Pairs well with Profile B (web search) or Profile C (tenant docs).

**Spine emphasis:** context (sources), format (key findings + supporting evidence), exclusions (no unverified claims).

**Example input:** "Synthesize the last six analyst reports on the GLP-1 market into a one-page brief."

---

### 4. Presentation deck outline

**Use case:** Slide-by-slide outline for a deck, not the slides themselves. The Minto pyramid applied.

**Spine emphasis:** format (insight titles per slide), exclusions (no descriptive titles, no slide with >35 words of body).

**Example input:** "12-slide outline for my board update on the AU/NZ expansion."

---

### 5. Data analysis request

**Use case:** Frame a data question for the LLM to answer (or for a human analyst, if Profile A).

**Spine emphasis:** format (question + expected output shape), critique ("does the answer actually inform the decision?").

**Example input:** "Help me understand whether our pricing has drifted vs. competition in the last 18 months."

## Domain-specific archetypes (the niche wedge)

These five exploit Joel Robic's domain authority and create the LinkedIn-network GTM. No marketplace competitor has them.

### 6. Pharma regulatory submission draft

**Use case:** Cover letters, regulatory correspondence, briefing documents for FDA, Health Canada, EMA, TGA, MHRA submissions.

**Spine emphasis:** role (senior regulatory affairs strategist), format (formal regulatory structure), exclusions (no superiority claims without evidence, no internal codenames, regulatory-lawyer precision).

**Example input:** "Draft a Health Canada NDS cover letter for our new oral antidiabetic."

**Capability profile sweet spot:** C (tenant access to prior submissions, clinical data, regulatory feedback emails).

---

### 7. Biotech investor update (quarterly cadence)

**Use case:** The recurring quarterly update letter to investors. Pipeline progress, catalysts ahead, capital position, candor on setbacks.

**Spine emphasis:** role (CFO + IR director), format (structured: pipeline / financials / outlook), exclusions (no hype, no buried bad news, no boilerplate).

**Example input:** "Q3 investor update for our oncology biotech — Phase 2 readout coming Q1."

**Capability profile sweet spot:** C (clinical data, prior letters for tone match, board meeting notes).

---

### 8. Due-diligence quick look (M&A, investment)

**Use case:** First-pass diligence on a target — company, asset, or therapeutic program. Decision-grade summary in under 24 hours.

**Spine emphasis:** role (M&A analyst + scientific lead), format (commercial / clinical / IP / regulatory / financial), exclusions (no false confidence, flag what you couldn't verify).

**Example input:** "Quick-look diligence on [target] — they're pitching us next Tuesday."

**Capability profile sweet spot:** B (web search for public filings, news) or C (internal precedent on similar deals).

---

### 9. Post-incident review (compliance, quality, safety)

**Use case:** Structured review after a deviation, audit finding, or quality incident. Root cause, corrective actions, preventive measures.

**Spine emphasis:** role (quality / compliance lead), format (incident summary / root cause / CAPA / timeline), exclusions (no blame attribution, no speculation without evidence).

**Example input:** "Post-incident review for the warehouse temperature excursion last Friday."

**Capability profile sweet spot:** C (related incident history, SOPs, audit trail in tenant).

---

### 10. Board-ready strategic brief (regulated industry)

**Use case:** A one-pager for the board on a specific strategic decision. Recommendation, evidence, risks, ask.

**Spine emphasis:** role (chief of staff + senior strategy partner), format (recommendation up front, then evidence, then ask), exclusions (no hedging, no consulting-speak, every claim sourced).

**Example input:** "Board brief on whether we should partner with [Co] or build internally for the Australia launch."

**Capability profile sweet spot:** C (organizational context, prior board materials, decision precedent).

---

## Validation rule (from Phase 0.5)

After ten user interviews:
- Any archetype circled by **<3 of 10** users → drop from V1.
- Any task mentioned by **≥3 of 10** users that isn't on this list → add to V1 (or V1.5 if effort is high).

The list is provisional until Phase 0.5 synthesis. Treat it as a starting hypothesis, not a final decision.
